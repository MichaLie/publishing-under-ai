Publishing Under AI (2025–2026)

Prose is cheap now. Check what’s behind it.

Now that fluent prose costs almost nothing, a well-written paper no longer shows that the work behind it was done well. Trust has to come from what can be checked: data, code, references that resolve, and a named person who answers for each claim.

This page puts 141 studies, editorials, policies and public posts side by side: what has measurably changed in scientific writing and peer review, seven questions where researchers and editors disagree, and eight fixes proposed or tried by journals, conferences, preprint servers and funders, with how each can fail.

01 · Measured shifts

What has measurably changed

≥13.5% · 89% 2024 abstracts vs. Dec 2025 PMC
SW1 · Drafting

Distinct estimators put AI-assisted writing at ≥13.5% of 2024 PubMed abstracts and 89% of December 2025 PMC full texts

Excess-vocabulary tracking across 15M+ PubMed abstracts placed 2024 LLM-assisted prevalence at ≥13.5%, while a newer estimator applied to structured English-language PubMed Central full texts estimates 89% in December 2025 (and 68% in Discussion versus 32% in Methods on random 255-word crops). Because the corpora, text units, and statistical estimators differ, the two figures measure distinct slices of biomedical writing.

2.1M papers Signal inversion & style compression
SW2 · Drafting

Prose complexity flips its observational association with review outcomes; separately, LLM rewrites compress stylistic variance

Across 2.1M preprints and 28K peer reviews, linguistic complexity showed opposite observational associations with later peer-reviewed publication and review scores in manuscripts classified as LLM-assisted versus undetected papers, while Renault et al. showed that first-detection adoption dating can mechanically inflate apparent productivity jumps. Separately, controlled rewrites of 1,000 arXiv abstracts and 1,000 Reddit stories narrowed writing-complexity variance across most tested models and prompts.

6.3 → 23.0 Papers with a flagged ref, per 10,000
SW3 · Citations

Papers with at least one possibly fabricated reference climb from 6.3 to 23.0 per 10,000 (2023–Q1 2026)

Screening 500,000 peer-reviewed papers in chemistry, physics, materials science, engineering and computer science found papers with at least one possibly fabricated reference rising from 6.3 per 10,000 in 2023 to 23.0 per 10,000 in the first quarter of 2026 (85.8% precision on masked checks; ‘fabricated’ means the reference could not be verified). The same team's audit of 2.5 million biomedical papers in PubMed Central found 4,046 fabricated references in 2,810 papers, at a rate that rose more than twelvefold to about 57 per 10,000 papers by early 2026, and an audit of 111 million references across arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN and PubMed Central conservatively estimates 146,932 hallucinated citations in 2025 alone. At NeurIPS and USENIX Security 2025, about 1 in 20 accepted papers carried at least two suspect references before human checking, and most flags checked by hand proved recoverable or false. A Nature correspondence argues that confirmed hallucinated citations should count as misconduct.

0.1% public 76 / 75,172 full texts + hard caps
SW4 · Desk Triage

Public AI disclosures appear in ~0.1% of sampled papers and private ones in 3–6% of medical-journal submissions, while arXiv and AJPS cap submissions

Across 5,114 journals and 5.2 million papers, He and Bu found that about 70% of sampled Q1 journals had AI policies by January 2025, yet public writing-assistance statements appeared in only 76 of 75,172 sampled full texts published since 2023 (about 0.1%); a PNAS exchange debated how far private submission declarations change that picture. Private declarations at two medical publishers tell a similar story: 5.7% of 25,114 research manuscripts submitted to BMJ Group journals in 2024 and 3.3% of 105,538 manuscripts submitted to 13 JAMA Network journals (rising to about 6% by October 2025) disclosed AI use, far below the 28–76% of researchers who report using it in surveys. In autumn 2026, JACC reaffirmed its exemption for writing-only help, arXiv capped submitters at two papers per calendar month (a year after its computer-science section began requiring peer review for review and position papers), AJPS limited authors to three submissions in any rolling 12 months, and bioRxiv and medRxiv reserved the right to rate-limit authors.

50.5% accuracy Readers: authentic vs. AI-manipulated
SW5 · Desk Triage

Detected paper-mill output grows rapidly while PhD-level readers reach 50.5% accuracy telling authentic from selected AI-manipulated figures

Forensic network analysis finds suspected paper-mill output growing far faster than publications overall, and a classifier trained on retracted mill papers flags 9.87% of 2.6 million cancer-research papers as similar to mill output, a share that rose from about 1% of annual output in the early 2000s to more than 15% by the early 2020s, including in the top 10% of journals. Commentators warn that agentic AI now mines open cohorts such as NHANES and UK Biobank at speed. In a controlled study, 24 PhD-level biomedical readers judging authentic and selected AI-manipulated Western blot and tumour images were right 50.5% of the time on average.

12% – 21% Classifier-flagged reviews, three corpora
SW6 · Peer Review

Classifiers flag 12–21% of reviews as AI-written, and ICML catches 1% of reviews breaking a no-LLM pledge

One preprint classifier flags about 20% of sampled ICLR 2025 reviews and 12% of sampled 2025 Nature Communications reviews as AI-generated, and a detector vendor reports 21% of ICLR 2026 reviews as fully AI-generated. At ICML 2026, hidden watermark phrases caught 795 reviews (about 1%) by reviewers who had agreed not to use LLMs, and in an anonymous survey 22.5% of reviewers under the no-LLM policy said they had used one anyway. An editorial sets a Frontiers survey, in which 53% of reviewers reported using AI, beside an informal count at one journal where more than 95% declared non-use. Controlled experiments show AI reviews agreeing excessively with one another and AI scores rising after an LLM rewrites a paper, and detectors misfire: Pangram and GPTZero label 3.1% and 3.4% of human-written, AI-polished reviews as fully AI-generated.

53–96% · 26/30 Bounded RCT & editor-pair tests
SW7 · Peer Review

AI reviewers catch planted errors and win editors' blinded preference tests, yet a hidden sentence can flip their verdicts

In a clinical-trial preprint, 15 LLMs scored 53–96% on detecting major errors planted in three historical trial reports, against a 27% mean among journal peer reviewers in the original study, and 58–99% on recent trials (the top three models on three of 58), scored by LLM judges without new human comparators; models also flagged 14–29% of error categories in unmodified reports. At AAAI-26, all 22,977 papers that reached full review received an AI review, which surveyed authors and committee members preferred on six of nine criteria while finding it more prone to dwell on minor issues. In a social-science working paper, three former editors preferred AI reviews in 26 of 30 blinded judgments without reading the manuscripts. AI reviewers are also easy to steer: a hidden nudge flipped the accept-or-reject recommendations of four commercial models on medical manuscripts in 84% of cases, and warning them barely helped. A randomized ICLR trial tests a narrower role, feedback on human reviews.

53.7% → 81.5% LLM auditor with logs + code added
SW8 · Venues

AI Scientist enters Nature, ALIFE 2026 accepts a co-created paper, and PNAS shows why manuscript-only audits miss flaws

Alongside Nature's report on The AI Scientist (covering a human-filtered, protocol-withdrawn workshop run) and the reported acceptance at ALIFE 2026 of an Error Diffusion paper that human researchers produced using a development version of The Agentic Scientist, a 2026 PNAS study showed that giving an LLM-based auditor agent logs and code lifted its accuracy from 53.7% to 81.5% on a constructed flaw benchmark — complementing PaperBench's partial-credit rubric scores (21.0% for Claude 3.5 Sonnet with BasicAgent; 24.4–26.0% for o1 with IterativeAgent; not the share of papers replicated), ClaimNov's controlled novelty benchmark, and Paper2Agent's 74/100 MCP conversions.

What is a paper now?

When fluent scientific prose costs almost nothing to generate, does writing a manuscript still test scientific thinking, or has prose become a secondary view layer over data and code?

◀ Writing IS the thinking

Drafting prose forces authors to discover gaps in logic and own every claim; delegating text generation produces smooth paragraphs around half-formed ideas and shifts cognitive effort onto readers.

Executable package first ▶

The 5,000-word static essay compresses messy computation into narrative theatre; verified datasets, runnable code, and machine-checkable claims should be the primary record, with prose rendered per reader.

LLMs inside peer review

Should peer review stay a strictly human sanctuary, adopt structured AI co-review loops, or hand first-pass evaluation to models?

03 · Proposed models

Eight proposed fixes, and how each can fail

Ideas piloted or debated by journals, conferences, preprint servers and funders in 2025–2026.

M1 One large trial; pilots & proposals
F2F4

AI Review-Critic & Bounded "Reviewer 4" Loops

Keep human editors and referees accountable while deploying auditable AI for technical checks and review-quality feedback

Mechanism: Manuscripts stay under human editorial accountability while bounded AI tools run consistency, statistical, and reporting checks — either upstream for authors before submission or alongside human referees as a non-voting technical report and review-specificity coach.

Target payoff

Targets mechanical, arithmetic, and reporting omissions that time-pressed human referees miss while nudging vague two-line reviews toward concrete critique.

Where it breaks / gets gamed

Reviewers rubber-stamp the AI report, LLMs invent plausible flaw lists on strong papers, or unlogged desk tools penalize non-Anglophone manuscripts without human override tracking.

Concrete 2025–2026 pilots (4) & mapped sources (37)
  • ICLR 2025 / Nature Machine Intelligence randomized trial (~20,000 human reviews offered optional LLM feedback)
  • EMBO / Review Commons + qed author-centric pre-submission self-check survey and pilot (2026)
  • Nature Biotechnology "Reviewer 4" proposal, JAMA Network's stated plan to explore "driver-assistance" hybrid strategies, & OpenAI Prism Paper Review workflow (2025–2026)
  • Clinical-trial methodological error screeners tested on inserted RCT flaws (Brian et al. 2026 preprint)

M2 Deployed in screening
F2F4F6

Forensic Integrity Hubs & Adversarial Sanitization

Screen synthetic submissions, phantom citations, and prompt injections at manuscript ingestion — with human verification

Mechanism: Treats manuscript ingestion like cybersecurity with due process: flags invisible PDF prompt layers, cross-checks cited DOIs and titles against bibliographic registries, screens figures for duplication or generative artifacts, and routes candidate flags to human editors before any adverse decision.

Target payoff

Flags candidate phantom citations and crude prompt injections before volunteer referee hours are spent.

Where it breaks / gets gamed

Selected AI-manipulated Western blots and xenograft figures fool PhD readers near chance (50.5%), semantic rewrites evade surface filters, and unverified desk flags risk false accusations without transparent author appeal routes.

Concrete 2025–2026 pilots (4) & mapped sources (31)
  • STM Integrity Hub & publisher paper-mill / review-mill screening pipelines
  • Screening for hidden white-font or micro-font PDF text aimed at LLM reviewers (recommended; a flagging tool for CHI 2027 is described secondhand)
  • Automated reference-existence cross-checks against bibliographic databases (~$0.04/paper API cost in one venue-scale scan, before human adjudication)
  • Proposed due-process safeguards for misconduct screening, with human verification and author reply (Hosseini & Resnik 2024/2025)

M3 Selectively piloted
F3F4F7

Costly-Signal & Scarcity Restoration

Reintroduce human bandwidth limits where prose fluency no longer filters effort

Mechanism: Because polished prose no longer signals scarce human labor, venues shift screening to resources that models cannot mint infinitely: rolling per-author submission budgets and capped human-reviewed tracks.

Target payoff

Aims to cap zero-marginal-cost submission surges directly (effectiveness untested; arXiv calls its limit a stopgap) without relying on brittle AI-text classifiers or token watermarks.

Where it breaks / gets gamed

Flat co-author caps squeeze large interdisciplinary labs and early-career collaborators.

Concrete 2025–2026 pilots (3) & mapped sources (22)
  • American Journal of Political Science (AJPS) rolling 12-month cap of 3 submissions per author or co-author, with one annual waiver request (enacted 4 Nov 2026)
  • arXiv limit of 2 submissions per calendar month per submitter from October 2026 (up to 3 active submissions, a limit in place since 2024)
  • Individual proposal ahead of ICML 2027 for a human-reviewed conference track with a hard per-author cap beside an uncapped AI-reviewed track (Yona 2026)

M4 Early pilots & proposals
F1F3F7

Process Provenance ("Research Flight Recorders")

Move past binary AI-prose checkboxes toward workflow traces, raw-data archives, and formal verification

Mechanism: Audits how a study unfolded across hypotheses, raw instrument files, rejected runs, and formal proofs so editors and readers can inspect analytical choices that a polished manuscript PDF conceals.

Target payoff

Targets post-hoc selection bias, undocumented data modifications, and low-information AI checkboxes (in one PNAS study of two AI-scientist systems, an LLM auditor's accuracy on a constructed benchmark rose from 53.7% to 81.5% when given logs and code).

Where it breaks / gets gamed

High tooling friction outside computational fields; exploratory wet-lab science looks messy when surveilled commit-by-commit, and long-term raw-file repositories require sustained funder support.

Concrete 2025–2026 pilots (4) & mapped sources (29)
  • End-to-end agent log traces, full hypothesis pools, and code-execution audits tested in PNAS (Luo, Kasirzadeh & Shah 2026) and proposed in Cell (Guo et al. 2026)
  • Funded archives of raw images, instrument outputs, and provenance metadata proposed in Nature (Shi, Nie & Kong 2026)
  • Proposed four-dimension AI disclosure in Pediatric Research (Coppes et al. 2026) & the proposed Resnik–Hosseini three-tier materiality framework vs. JACC's existing writing-only exemption (Krumholz 2026)
  • Registered Reports (Stage 1 pre-data acceptance) and Lean formal proof verification in mathematics and theoretical CS

M5 Prototypes & early tools
F1F5F7

Executable & Modular Research Packages

Treat static prose as a view layer over runnable code, primary data, and interactive paper-agents

Mechanism: Packages data, computational environments, code, and structured claim graphs alongside the narrative text so continuous integration and downstream readers can execute and inspect methods directly.

Target payoff

Targets computational irreproducibility and separates executable verification from rhetorical polish, while human experts still guide open-ended biological validation. (For scale, on OpenAI's from-scratch PaperBench benchmark the best-scoring unaided agents reached 21.0–26.0% mean partial credit when re-implementing papers.)

Where it breaks / gets gamed

Wet-lab, clinical, qualitative, and field sciences cannot reduce experimental validity to container re-execution, and agreement with reference outputs checks faithful execution without proving underlying scientific truth.

Concrete 2025–2026 pilots (3) & mapped sources (16)
  • Paper2Agent (Nature 2026) converting 74 of 100 computational-biology papers into Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers and interactive workflows
  • A partner's prototype for eLife mapping claims to evidence across ten of its neuroscience papers (2026)
  • Executable MyST / Curvenote / Jupyter-native articles

M6 Live ecosystem
F2F6F7

Publish-Review-Curate (PRC) + Open Audit Bots

Preprint openly, run specialized technical check-bots with human verification, and curate via editors

Mechanism: Separates rapid open preprint dissemination from binary accept/reject gates; specialized screeners inspect code, proofs, reference tables, and citations while human editors verify flags and curate conceptual significance.

Target payoff

Aims to reduce multi-journal rejection cascades where one flawed or synthetic manuscript burns several closed referee pools in sequence.

Where it breaks / gets gamed

University hiring and grant panels still weight legacy journal mastheads, and broadcasting unverified bot flags risks reputational harm when models misread domain notation (F6).

Concrete 2025–2026 pilots (3) & mapped sources (18)
  • eLife PRC model, Review Commons, PCI (Peer Community In), and Genomics x AI rapid community blog
  • QED Science scoring a year's worth of life-science preprints (57,455), by the developer's account (2026)
  • Specialized replication, formula, and reference screeners (Astra code-package auditor, GPT-6.1 Sol arXiv math scans, Problematic Paper Screener)

M7 Live 2025–2026 experiment
F1F2F7

Dedicated Agent-Native Venues & Sandboxes

Give autonomous AI research systems an explicit, labeled track for open stress-testing

Mechanism: Establishes dedicated conferences and journals explicitly labeled for AI-authored or AI-reviewed work so autonomous pipelines can be audited openly outside covert human-only queues.

Target payoff

Provides a transparent sandbox for benchmarking agentic workflows, claim-level novelty filters (ClaimNov), and methodological failure modes without covert rule-breaking.

Where it breaks / gets gamed

Critics warn dedicated AI venues normalize synthetic volume ("slop") or obscure heavy human steering behind nominal AI-authorship branding.

Concrete 2025–2026 pilots (3) & mapped sources (11)
  • Stanford Agents4Science 2025 (>300 submissions, 48 accepted papers with AI first authors and AI reviewers on OpenReview)
  • Journal of AI Generated Papers (JAIGP, jaigp.org, launched by César A. Hidalgo in Feb 2026)
  • Sakana AI Scientist (Yamada et al. 2025; Lu et al. Nature 2026 — same human-filtered, protocol-withdrawn ICLR workshop test) & The Agentic Scientist (an Error Diffusion paper produced by human researchers using a development version, reported accepted at ALIFE 2026)

M8 Institutional experiment
F1F4F7

Institutional Journal Exit

Funders and institutes mandate open modular outputs and opt out of legacy journals

Mechanism: Research funders remove the commercial journal incentive for their grantees — declining to pay APCs to publishers and funding immediate open datasets, code, and modular reports instead.

Target payoff

Bypasses journal APCs, multi-month review queues, and prestige-driven narrative packaging for funded grantees.

Where it breaks / gets gamed

Early-career researchers returning to conventional university departments face hiring and grant friction without journal lines, and self-hosted reports can struggle to attract independent external peer scrutiny.

Concrete 2025–2026 pilots (3) & mapped sources (5)
  • Astera Institute policy ending funding and permission for traditional journal articles (in effect by June 2025)
  • Arcadia Science open modular publishing experiment (lessons analyzed 2023–2025)
  • Open hypothesis and modular release platforms bypassing journal cycles (2025–2026)

About this collection

What it is, and what it is not

A curated map of 141 sources on generative AI in scientific writing, peer review and research integrity, almost all from 2025 and 2026: empirical studies and preprints, journal editorials, integrity investigations, policy announcements, and public posts by researchers and editors.
